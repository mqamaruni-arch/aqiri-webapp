Research Project

Halal Certification and the Development of Shari'a-Compliant Products and Services in Nigeria: Shari'a Regulations, Awareness and Economic Implication

Research conducted byAbba A. Dandago

Research Fellow, AQIRI

Research Abstract

Abstract

Halal certification has become a global quality-assurance and market-access mechanism, and the global halal economy spans food, finance, pharmaceuticals, cosmetics, tourism and logistics with almost $7.7 trillion. Nigeria has Africa’s largest Muslim population and a fast-growing consumer market where the halal market may reach $1.5 billion by 2030 as estimated, yet its halal ecosystem remains fragmented. Certification is handled by a mix of religious bodies, private firms and foreign certifiers; statutory regulators (NAFDAC, SON, CBN, NAICOM, SEC) address halal only indirectly; and consumer and producer awareness is uneven.

This study will examine (i) the Shari’a and statutory regulatory framework governing halal certification and Shari’a-compliant products and services in Nigeria, (ii) the level and determinants of awareness among consumers, producers and regulators, and (iii) the economic implications of certification for firms, consumers and national export competitiveness.

A convergent mixed-methods design will combine doctrinal (usul al-fiqh and legal) analysis, document analysis, a cross-sectional survey, and semi-structured interviews and focus groups across Nigeria’s six geopolitical zones. The study will propose a harmonised, Maqasid al-Shari’a-based national halal governance model.
