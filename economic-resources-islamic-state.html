Research Project

The Economic Resources of the Islamic State in the Works on Kharāj and Siyāsah Sharʿiyyah and Their Impact on Achieving Comprehensive Development in the Modern State

Research conducted byIbraheem Abdulwahab Abdulkareem

Research Fellow, AQIRI

Research Abstract

Abstract

Economic and financial thought in the Islamic state rests on an integrated conception of human stewardship (istikhlāf) that seeks to maximize resources, distribute them wisely, and achieve comprehensive development and the flourishing of human civilization. This thesis aims to establish a methodological and conceptual foundation for the study of economic resources through the texts of works on kharāj and siyāsah sharʿiyyah. It goes beyond reductionist readings that treat the Islamic financial system purely as a system of taxation. The study addresses a central question. How did works on kharāj and siyāsah sharʿiyyah lay the foundations for an integrated economic and conceptual approach to the governance of resources, and to what extent can this historical model be used to address the challenges of sustainable development and contemporary financial governance in the modern state?

The research combines an inductive method for analyzing historical texts, a descriptive method for examining the current state of development, a comparative method for comparing historical financial instruments with contemporary systems, and an applied method for putting principles into practice. The study is organized into two main parts. The first examines economic resources and the mechanisms for collecting them. It draws a clear distinction between sovereign and recurrent revenues, such as zakāh, kharāj, and fayʾ, and natural assets, including land, water, and minerals. It also explains the institutional organization of the public treasury (bayt al-māl) and the rules governing investment. The second part examines the developmental impact of these resources. It reviews the intellectual [word incomplete in the Arabic text] of comprehensive development in its economic, social, and environmental dimensions and presents practical and forward-looking models for addressing contemporary crises such as budget deficits, inflation, and public debt.
